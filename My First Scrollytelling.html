Part 1 · Before

Scrolling only moves things

On a normal page the words slide up and that's the whole trick. Scroll fast or slow, the page can't tell the difference.

It has no idea where you are in the story. That's the part scrollytelling fixes.

see the lines drifting? same lines, forever.

Part 2 · The idea

Now the picture starts paying attention

Look at the sky. It's night, and the sun is hiding behind the hills. As you scroll through this card, the sun climbs and the sky turns to day.

Scroll back up and the sun sinks again. You're not watching an animation. You're steering one, with your thumb.

this is the moment I got excited.

Part 2 · The idea

One scroll position, one frame

Here's the secret. Your scroll position is just a number. If you chop a picture into frames, each number can pick a frame, like a flipbook.

Scroll through this card and watch the little plant grow. Five frames, and the slider shows exactly where you are.

flipbooks, but for grown-ups.

Part 3 · Making one

Two ideas hold it all together

Idea one. Stick the picture to the screen with a CSS rule called position: sticky. The words scroll past and the picture stays put.

Idea two. A short script asks which block of words is in the middle of the screen, then tells the picture which scene to show.

that's honestly it. the rest is drawing.

Part 3 · Making one

Mistakes I made along the way

  • My first version had words sitting on top of other words.
  • On my phone the picture covered the text I was trying to read.
  • The page scrolled sideways once, and I never found out why.
  • I made everything animate. It was awful.

less moving stuff = better, I think.

Part 3 · Making one

Test it on a phone

Most people will read this on a phone, so build that version first and stretch it for big screens afterwards.

Scroll and watch the phone change. First the picture sits on top of the words. Then I move it up and give the words their own space.

do this before you show anyone.

If you want to make your own

  1. Pick a story with a beginning, a middle and an end. If you can't tell it out loud in a minute, it's too big for a first try.
  2. Cut it into short steps. One idea each, a few sentences at most.
  3. Choose one picture per step. Sketch them on paper before you open any code.
  4. Build the sticky stage first, then add the steps. Get one scene working before you start the second.
  5. Test on a phone, then in dark mode. Fix what looks wrong, not what looks fine.
  6. Cut anything that moves for no reason. Movement should always carry the story.

Words I learned

Scrollytelling
Telling a story where scrolling changes what's on the screen.
Scene
One picture the story shows for a while. This page has ten.
Step
One block of words. When it reaches the middle of the screen, its scene appears.
Sticky
A CSS setting that pins something to the screen while everything around it scrolls.
Viewport
The part of the page you can see right now.
Scrolljacking
When a site takes over scrolling so it stops feeling like yours. Critics of scrollytelling worry about this a lot.
